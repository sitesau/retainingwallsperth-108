Approval questions: does the 0.5 m exemption apply to my wall

The most common approval question is whether a permit is needed at all. In Western Australia the building permit exemption generally stops at 0.5 m of retained ground. Below that line, many garden walls proceed without formal approval. Above it, or where the wall connects to other building work, proper approval is needed.

  • Measure retained ground, not the visible face. A wall buried into a slope may hold back more soil than its exposed height suggests.
  • Watch the connected-work triggers. A wall tied to a boundary fence, building pad, paving or excavation can move into approval territory even at lower heights.
  • Check adjoining land. Where a wall supports ground near a boundary, the conversation should include the neighbour's side and any fence that shares the load.
  • Ask about engineering early. For taller or structural walls, ask whether AS 4678 design and engineering sign-off apply, and confirm the pathway with local government before work starts.

Cost questions: what hides behind the metre rate

Perth owners comparing retaining wall contractors perth answers on price are usually reacting to a single number. The more useful question is what the number includes. Guide figures of $250-$600 per m2 for limestone block and $450-$700 per m2 for concrete post and panel come before excavation, drainage and engineering, so two quotes with similar metre rates can describe very different jobs.

  • Ask for excavation and spoil removal as their own line items.
  • Ask whether ag-drain, gravel and geofabric sit inside the rate or outside it.
  • Ask how access limits, such as tight side passages or sloped sites, change the price.
  • Ask what happens if rock, wet ground or unsuitable soil appears once digging begins.

Timber sleepers suit lower garden terraces and boundary retaining, while besser block is core-filled and steel-reinforced for taller engineered walls. Rock and boulder walls are usually battered back on sloped Perth Hills blocks. None of these is an interchangeable finish, so a bare per-metre comparison across different systems rarely means much.

Drainage questions: why walls lean after winter

Perth's wet winters and dry summers push water behind walls and then pull the ground dry, and that cycle is behind many failures. Ag-drain, gravel and geofabric behind every wall should be treated as structure, not an optional extra to trim once the quote lands.

  • Ask where water will go once it reaches the drainage layer, and where it discharges legally.
  • Ask how backfill is separated from the soil so fines do not clog the drainage path.
  • Ask whether the drainage detail changes for limestone, post and panel, timber sleeper or engineered walls.

For an existing wall, warning signs include leaning, bulging, cracking, gaps opening at the base, water leaking through the face after rain, and soil settling above the wall. Repair talks should start with why the wall moved, because replacing the surface without fixing the cause can simply repeat the failure.

Contractor questions: how to compare quotes fairly

A sound quote names the wall type, retained height, length, excavation assumptions, access limits, drainage build-up and the engineering pathway where relevant. It also explains how variations are handled if site conditions differ after excavation starts.

  1. Describe the block accurately: length, retained height, slope, soil character and nearby structures.
  2. Check approval risk against the 0.5 m line, adjoining land and any connected work.
  3. Compare complete scopes, matching excavation, drainage, material and engineering like for like.
  4. Ask what signals would make repair unsuitable compared with replacement.

This suits anyone planning a new wall, replacing a failed one, levelling part of a sloped block, building a garden terrace or checking whether a boundary or building-pad wall needs formal approval. It also helps owners handed a simple metre rate who need to know what may sit outside that headline number.
